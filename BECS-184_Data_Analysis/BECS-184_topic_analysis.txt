BECS-184 : DATA ANALYSIS
Topic Frequency Analysis - TEE papers Dec 2022 - June 2026 (8 papers)
========================================================================

EXAM FORMAT (stable across all 8 papers)
----------------------------------------
3 hours | 100 marks | TWO sections:
  Section A: answer any 2 of 4 questions x 20 marks = 40
  Section B: answer any 5 of ~7-8 questions x 12 marks = 60
The LAST question (Q11/Q12) is ALWAYS "short notes, any 3 of ~5, 12 marks"
- a guaranteed marks pocket with a highly predictable pool.
=> Massive built-in choice: you answer 7 questions out of ~12 offered.

PATTERN OBSERVATIONS
--------------------
* Question SHAPES repeat more than exact wording:
  - "What is X? Explain construction/procedure. Illustrate." (composite
    index, histogram, hypothesis testing)
  - "Distinguish between A and B" (point/interval, skew/kurtosis,
    correlation/causation, primary/secondary, null/alternative, uni/multi,
    census/survey, bar/histogram) - the most common 12-mark template
  - "Compute r / mean / SD from this small table" - ~15 marks, formulaic
* Verbatim-ish repeats across consecutive sessions:
  - Primary-data-collection methods: Dec24, Jun25, Dec25
  - Composite index construction: Dec23, Dec24, Jun24, Dec25, Jun26 (5/8!)
  - Histogram from frequency table: Dec23, Dec25 (Jun25 as procedure)
  - Graphical presentation importance: Dec24 (20m), Jun26 (20m)
  - Multivariate techniques brief: Dec23 (20m), Dec25 (12m), Jun26 (20m)
  - Mean/median/mode relationship + suitability: Dec22, Dec25
  - Correlation-causation: Jun23, Jun24, Dec25
* 2025-26 trend: sets & logic entered Section A as a 20-marker (Jun25,
  Jun26 SNs); regression (Y=a+bX) appeared Jun26; HDI named in composite
  index question. The paper has shifted slightly toward formal
  math-fundamentals alongside classic statistics.

MARKS RATIO (by marks offered, 8-paper average)
-----------------------------------------------
 ~25% numerical/computational (correlation, mean/SD, histogram draw,
     harmonic mean, percentile, average price, growth-rate average)
 ~20% mixed (define + properties + compute; define + illustrate)
 ~55% pure theory/explain/distinguish
NOTE: the numericals are all FORMULA-PLUG questions (r, mean, SD) - far
simpler than BCS-042 traces. Learn 4 formulas, cover every numerical
in the paper. The "distinguish" template answers are semi-mechanical:
2 columns x 4-5 rows of differences.

TOP-PRIORITY PREP LIST (covers ~75% of any paper)
--------------------------------------------------
 1. Central tendency: definitions, formulas, mean-median-mode
    relationship (symmetric: mean=median=mode; skewed: mode < median
    < mean), when median/mode/geometric mean is suitable
 2. Composite index: 6-step construction + simple ranking method +
    range equalisation vs mean standardisation + HDI/PQLI as examples
 3. Pearson correlation: formula (compute from table!), 5 properties,
    significance test with t-table, correlation vs causation
 4. Regression: Y=a+bX, how to find a & b (least squares normal
    equations), properties of regression coefficients
 5. Histogram + graph types: importance, when to use which, draw
    histogram from frequency table, bar vs histogram, frequency polygon
 6. Distinguish-pair pack (each = 4-5 row table):
    primary/secondary data, sampling/non-sampling errors, census/survey,
    point/interval estimation, null/alternative hypothesis, uni/multi-
    variate, skewness/kurtosis, correlation/causation
 7. Hypothesis testing: 5 steps + Type I/II + p-value method
 8. Multivariate techniques brief: multiple regression, discriminant
    analysis, factor analysis, cluster analysis, MANOVA, multidimensional
    scaling (one line each) + interpretation cautions
 9. Primary data collection methods (observation, interview, question-
    naire, schedule, case study) + secondary data sources & limits
10. Qualitative analysis methods: content analysis, grounded theory,
    thematic analysis, narrative analysis, FGD handling
11. Short-notes pack: coefficient of determination, FGD, non-sampling
    errors, secondary sources, normal distribution properties, random
    variable, types of data, snowball sampling, standard error, CLT
12. Sets: definition, representation, operations (union/intersection/
    complement/difference), properties - 1 worked example each
